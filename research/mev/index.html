Where the MEV comes from

The user’s earlier swap makes ETH more expensive in Pool A. A searcher can capture the gap by getting a trade included after it. Set the earlier trade to 0 USDC and watch the opportunity disappear.

Path finding

The lab checks two- and three-swap loops that start and end in USDC, using each pool at most once. The token path and the choice of pools both matter.

Amount sizing

Every swap changes the reserves, so twice the input yields less than twice the output. Net profit is the USDC you get back minus your starting USDC and gas. The swap fees are already included in what you get back.

The four modeled pools use constant-product pricing with a 0.30% fee per swap. The size calculation uses continuous amounts and assumes the searcher can fund the route. The swaps in a route execute together in one transaction.
